City twins

Every city has a twin somewhere. Search yours.

    How it works

    The 11,403 cities are the urban centres of 50,000 people or more in the European Commission’s Urban Centre Database. A twin is a city at least 50 km away in another country with the same climate type (Köppen-Geiger), the closest average temperature and seasonal temperature spread, and a similar population; where possible within 2 °C of average temperature, 1 °C of seasonal spread and 1.5 times the population, and the card says “Same climate” and “similar size” only when that holds. The card then shows the clearest difference between the two, only where it is large enough to trust: population change since 1975, average building height, or greenness (the mean NDVI of the greener built-up land). Temperature and seasonal spread were checked against independent climate data interpolated from weather stations (WorldClim 2.1); rainfall and density were left out because they did not hold up. All population figures, 2025 included, are modelled estimates. A twin is a statistical likeness, not a claim that two places are the same.

    Limits: a twin is a statistical match on climate and size, not a judgement that two cities are alike in other ways. Temperatures are averages for the 2010s over each urban centre, all population figures, 2025 included, are modelled, and building height (2020) and greenness (2025) are satellite estimates. Rainfall is not used, because the database’s values did not agree with station records.

    Data: GHS Urban Centre Database R2024A V1.2, European Commission, Joint Research Centre, doi:10.2905/1a338be6, CC BY 4.0; 19 centres left out (14 without a name, 5 with missing values). Satellite views: Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX IT Services GmbH (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2024), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
