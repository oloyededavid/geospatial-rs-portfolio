Undergraduate Thesis+

Spatio-Temporal Assessment of Urban Heat Island Dynamics in Lagos State, Nigeria (2015–2024)

Final Year Research Project · Dept. of Remote Sensing & GIS, FUTA · Supervisor: Dr Akinola Komolafe

2024 – 2025

Lagos is one of Africa's fastest-growing megacities — and one of its hottest. This study set out to answer a pressing question: how has a decade of rapid urban expansion reshaped the thermal environment of Nigeria's commercial capital?

Using Landsat 7, 8, and 9 imagery (2015–2024), Land Surface Temperature (LST) was retrieved via the mono-window algorithm. Spectral indices — NDVI, NDWI, and NDBI — were computed to characterise vegetation cover, water bodies, and built-up density. LULC change was mapped through supervised Random Forest classification in Google Earth Engine and QGIS.

Results revealed a statistically significant increase in mean LST of +2.4°C across Lagos between 2015 and 2024, concentrated in high-density urban cores (Lagos Island, Surulere, Mushin). UHI intensity was strongly correlated with NDBI (r = 0.81) and inversely correlated with NDVI (r = −0.76). Peri-urban zones including Banana Island and Lekki Phase 1 showed localised cooling effects attributed to managed green spaces and water proximity. Over 34% of vegetated land in the study area was converted to impervious surfaces over the decade, directly amplifying thermal stress in affected communities.

Findings offer actionable insight for Lagos urban planners — prioritising green infrastructure, urban forestry and cool-roof policies in high-UHI zones could meaningfully reduce heat exposure for millions of residents.
