City twins

Every city has a twin somewhere. Search yours.

    How it works

    The 11,403 cities are the urban centres of 50,000 people or more in the European Commission’s Urban Centre Database. A twin is a city at least 50 km away in another country with the same climate type (Köppen-Geiger), the closest average temperature and seasonal temperature spread, and a similar population; where possible within 2 °C of average temperature, 1 °C of seasonal spread and 1.5 times the population, and the card says “Same climate” and “similar size” only when that holds. The card then shows the clearest difference between the two, only where it is large enough to trust: population change since 1975, average building height, or greenness (the mean NDVI of the greener built-up land). Temperature and seasonal spread were checked against independent climate data interpolated from weather stations (WorldClim 2.1); rainfall and density were left out because they did not hold up. Population before 2025 is modelled. A twin is a statistical likeness, not a claim that two places are the same.

    Data: GHS Urban Centre Database R2024A V1.2, European Commission, Joint Research Centre, doi:10.2905/1a338be6, CC BY 4.0; 19 centres left out (14 without a name, 5 with missing values). Satellite views: Sentinel-2 cloudless by EOX IT Services GmbH (contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2024), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.

    I hold a Bachelor of Technology in Remote Sensing and Geoscience Information Systems from the Federal University of Technology, Akure, earned in 2025 with First Class Honours in the top 4% of the School of Earth and Mineral Sciences, as a Nigerian National Petroleum Company Foundation Geoscience Undergraduate Scholar.

    Before that I was an undergraduate research assistant and student team lead at the Centre for Space Research and Applications, where I led four students and compared field temperature transects in Akure with satellite estimates; a research assistant in the Remote Sensing and GIS Lab; a geospatial analyst at GISPlus Technologies; and a geospatial intern with the Federal Airports Authority of Nigeria.

    My own projects weigh the water stored in the Lake Chad basin with gravity satellites, assess floods, droughts and design rainfall in two Nigerian river systems where gauges are missing, test satellite estimates of groundwater recharge, follow the Ogun River to the lagoon, trace urban heat across Lagos, map land cover in Kenya’s Mau Forest, test access to health care in Mozambique, and serve their outputs through an open data catalogue. Each states its uncertainty and what it was and was not checked against, and publishes its code and data so that others can check it.
