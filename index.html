About

I’m Oloyede David, a geospatial and environmental researcher who reads a changing Earth from above.

I trained in remote sensing and geoscience information systems at the Federal University of Technology, Akure, graduating in 2025 with First Class Honours in the top 4% of the School of Earth and Mineral Sciences, and I work across the whole field rather than one corner of it: water and hazards, climate and cities, land, agriculture and ecosystems. I have weighed the water stored in the Lake Chad basin with gravity satellites, reconstructed floods, droughts and extreme rainfall across Nigeria where gauges are missing, tested satellite estimates of groundwater recharge, followed the Ogun River from its headwaters to the lagoon, and traced a decade of urban heat across Lagos; beyond those studies I have mapped coastal floods with radar, assessed flood vulnerability, classified ten years of land cover change, compared field temperature transects with satellite measurements, and assisted with RTK and drone surveys in the field. The tools change with the question, from multispectral, thermal and radar imagery to gravimetry, spatial statistics and machine learning, but the standard does not: every result is checked against independent data where it exists, its uncertainty is stated plainly, and its code and data are published so that anyone can run it again. Water, climate, cities, farms and forests are, to me, one system seen from different angles, and I want to keep working across all of them, with Earth observation and GeoAI as the means and questions that matter to people as the end.
